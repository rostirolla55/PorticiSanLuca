The Inscription and its Decoding

This inscription is a classic example of Baroque or Neoclassical Latin epigraphy, likely located in a monumental or academic context. It is a solemn dedication combining religious devotion with academic and noble pride.

The first part of the text celebrates the patron:

  • CHRISTO PONT. OPTIMO: "To Christ, the Best Pontiff", the main dedication to Jesus Christ as high priest.

  • AUGUSTAE VM MATRI: "To the august Virgin Mother" (Virgini Mariae).

  • IPPOLITO ABBA FORNASARIUS BON. G. NOB.: "Ippolito Abbot Fornasari, Bolognese, Of Noble Lineage".

  • EN PATR. ARCHIGYM. IC DE MANE PRIM. EMER. D.: "Behold, the Patrician, Jurisconsult of the morning in the first place of the Archiginnasio, emeritus, dedicated".

Ippolito Fornasari was an illustrious jurist and professor between the late 17th and early 18th centuries. The phrase "DE MANE PRIM." confirms his academic status, referring to the morning chair, which was the most prestigious at the Archiginnasio.

The Poetic Composition (1693)

The second part of the inscription is a poem in elegiac couplets from the year 1693:

  • Eminet Illa Caput Tuscorum in Vertice Montis: "She [the Church] stands out above the Head of the Tuscans, on the summit of the mountain".

  • Illuc Productis Felsina Porticibus: "thither Bologna is led by the extended porticos".

  • Templi muro Hanc Virgo auget Domus Aurea Caeli / Porta Vigil Turris David in Hospitium: The Virgin, described with titles from the Litany of Loreto such as Domus Aurea and Turris David, welcomes the faithful into the Sanctuary.

  • Fitque sequens Ventrem Partus vum Virgine Civis / Filius utque Videns Optimus Excubitor: The citizen (Civis) metaphorically becomes a son (Filius) and a guardian (Excubitor) of the Virgin.

  • Augustae et Primi Regia Pontificum: Defines the Sanctuary as the palace of the Augusta and the first Pontiffs.

Historical Context: Gioseffo Carpegna

In 1693, Count Gioseffo Maria Carpegna held the position of Vice-Legate of Bologna. He represented the civil and administrative authority of the Papal States, acting as the right hand of the Cardinal Legate. His family, the Counts of Carpegna, was extremely influential in Rome, with ties to Cardinal Gaspare Carpegna. In official documents of the time, his name may appear Latinized as Iosephus Carpineus.
